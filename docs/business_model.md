# Botcierge: business model (draft)

Working name, rename freely. Feeds report section 3.

## Idea in one line
Robots-as-a-service for households and owner-run small premises. The customer describes a chore in plain language, adds a floor plan, budget and deadline. Botcierge decomposes that into a serviceable job spec, picks the robot, attachments and skill pack (trained policy), quotes it, then delivers, runs and collects the robot.

## The gap
| Today's options | Problem for a household |
|---|---|
| Buy a consumer robot (robot vacuum, lawn mower) | Single-purpose, upfront cost, idle most of the year, owner does setup |
| Hire a person (agency cleaner, part-time helper) | Cost and availability, scheduling, privacy for some tasks |
| Enterprise RaaS / robotics integrators | Sold B2B with long contracts; nobody sells capable robots per job to consumers |

Households don't know which robot fits a job, and they shouldn't need to. Capable robots (mobile manipulators, quadrupeds, humanoids) are too expensive to own for occasional chores but can be cheap per day when a fleet is shared.

## Value proposition
- **Customer:** "Tell us the chore, we send the right robot." No robotics knowledge needed. A transparent quote, a feasibility check against the deadline and budget, and edge cases flagged before booking.
- **USP:** the translation layer (task decomposition). Hardware is a commodity; matching a vague request to robot + attachments + policy conditioning is the hard part, and it improves with every job.

## Target segments (B2C)
1. Renters and owners doing move-in / move-out cleans (HDB and condo).
2. Landed homeowners: lawns, pools, multi-level cleaning, patrol while travelling.
3. Families with elderly relatives at home during working hours.
4. Hosts of home events (serving robots for a night).
5. Owner-run shops and home businesses (shophouse cleaning, after-hours patrol). Still sold as a consumer checkout, not a B2B contract.

## Revenue model
- Per-job rental: robot daily rate x days + add-on daily rates + skill pack flat fee + delivery/setup fee.
- Recurring plan (later): discounted weekly slots for repeat chores (lawn, cleaning).
- Damage-protection add-on at checkout.
- Promo codes for acquisition.

## Competitive advantage
1. Decomposition models trained on every job: acceptance/override data from customers and staff becomes training data.
2. Fleet utilisation: one robot serves many households, so per-day price beats ownership for occasional use.
3. Skill-pack catalogue: the same hardware sells many services by swapping policies.

## Marketing strategy
- Seasonal campaigns: move-out season, pre-Chinese New Year spring cleaning, school holidays (travel = patrol + lawn).
- Partnerships: property agents (move-in/out), condo management, eldercare referral.
- Short-form video of robots doing chores (high shareability).
- Referral credit and first-job promo (WELCOME10).
